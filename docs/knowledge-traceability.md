# Knowledge Traceability

## Purpose

This document defines how ideas, claims, and engineering practices presented in this book are traced back to their supporting evidence.

The objective is to distinguish between:

- Established knowledge
- Practical engineering experience
- Original proposals
- Research hypotheses

This allows readers to understand the confidence level and origin of every major concept.

---

# Core Principle

Every significant claim in the book should be traceable.

Traceability improves:

- Transparency
- Scientific integrity
- Reader trust
- Future maintainability

---

# Evidence Categories

## 1. Research-backed

The claim is supported by peer-reviewed research papers, academic books, standards, or well-established engineering literature.

Examples:

- Brooks (1987)
- ISO standards
- IEEE publications
- ACM papers

---

## 2. Practice-backed

The claim is based on engineering experience observed in real software projects.

Examples:

- Architecture reviews
- Production incidents
- Team workflows
- Industrial best practices

---

## 3. Author Proposal

The claim introduces a new framework, terminology, or engineering practice proposed by the authors.

Examples:

- Aegis
- Artifact Lifecycle Management
- Agentic Governance

These concepts should be clearly identified as original contributions.

---

## 4. Hypothesis

An idea that appears promising but requires further validation.

Hypotheses should never be presented as established facts.

Instead, they should encourage discussion and future research.

---

# Claim Classification

Whenever appropriate, important claims should include their evidence classification.

Example:

> Artifact Lifecycle Management is proposed as a general engineering model.

Evidence:

Author Proposal
